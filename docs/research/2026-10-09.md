# Prediction and drafting research — 09 October 2026

The tested candidate expands strategy coverage within the existing timer, rather than replacing DotaCaptain's formulas with a model trained on Valve Dota 2. This research does not establish a higher real PvP win rate. It identifies and tests a specific source of sensitivity in our configuration selector.

## Papers and implementations examined

| Primary source | Finding and application |
| --- | --- |
| [Chen et al., The Art of Drafting (2018)](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.10130), [author implementation](https://github.com/czxttkl/DraftArtist) | Drafting is treated as an alternating game with future-pick rollouts. The experiments compare **predicted** outcomes, not played-match victories. Its reward predictor used historical Dota 2 matches, so neither its weights nor reported gains transfer to DotaCaptain. The author code's `player.py`, `node.py` and `captain_mode_draft.py` implement selection, random legal rollouts, player-dependent reward backup, bans, and final action selection. The code includes a `0.2 * c` exploration multiplier: blindly copying a paper's exploration constant would not even reproduce this implementation. We retain native weighted drafting; a trustworthy, affordable rollout evaluator is needed before replacing it with MCTS. |
| [Cawley and Talbot, On Over-fitting in Model Selection (2010)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) | Selecting the best result on finite evaluation data can overfit the selection criterion. We separate plan selection from the nuisance-profile test cases used to assess the change. Item candidates must be compared on identical enemy cases, rather than mixing a three-preset score with a 64-plan score. |
| [Guo et al., On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks (2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) | Predictive confidence needs validation; classifier confidence can be miscalibrated. Here, equally weighted hypothetical opponents are not observations from the PvP population. Scenario fractions therefore remain counts, with no invented “win probability.” Temperature scaling would require appropriate observed, held-out outcomes; two incomplete match reports cannot support it. |
| [Akhmedov and Phan, Machine learning models for DOTA 2 outcomes prediction (2021)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01782) | This uses in-match time series and a gold-related prediction target. Its reported scores do not demonstrate pregame draft accuracy. Importing those scores, models or live features into a pregame DotaCaptain forecast would answer a different question. |

The library routes to hero identity, counters, synergy and item impact. The guide explains role balance, strategy fit, contextual counters and ordered purchases. These are useful inputs; a named counter alone is not an outcome guarantee.

## Tested change

Previous Quick: evaluate 64 own plans against three native enemy presets, then select by scenario wins and mean net worth.

Optional Experimental mode: perform that screen, then evaluate its top three plans against **all 64 enemy strategies**. Commit the cross-check only after all finalists finish on identical cases. Subsequent item search and strategy retuning use that expanded set. Auto and default Quick retain their previous selection policy because the confirmation batch regressed. Partial scout already enumerates compatible strategies; full scout remains fixed. Thorough already evaluates every own/enemy strategy pair and receives no extra finalist stage.

The initial screen is 192 simulations per known side; finalist cross-check adds 192. This is substantially smaller than Thorough's 4,096 before item search. Extra work can reduce the number of item candidates tried within the prep timer. The existing application reserve remains intact. An interrupted cross-check retains preset-based results and labels them accordingly; complete finalist scores are never mixed with incomplete or preset-only scores.

This covers strategy uncertainty for the tested roles/items. It does not cover every enemy role assignment, custom build, future hero composition or primary win condition. Equal weighting is a stress-test convention, not an estimated opponent distribution.

## Reproducible experiment

Run `node scripts/research-benchmark.cjs 6` for seeds 1701–1706. A separately seeded confirmation batch is `node scripts/research-benchmark.cjs 6 4701 shortlist-confirmation.json`.

Each legal 24-turn draft is generated using the unchanged native weighted drafting policy, alternating the tested faction. Own roles and default items remain fixed. Both selectors see the same team and selection scenarios. Each selected plan is then tested on the **same 32 separately seeded enemy profiles**: one of 64 plans plus a native-eligible role swap or default-purchase-order variant. These profiles are excluded from selection. The simulator's internal seed is unchanged. Results and composition IDs are recorded in the adjacent JSON files.

Initial batch: baseline **89/192**, cross-check **105/192** scenario wins; three drafts improved, three tied, none declined by scenario-win count. This is 16 additional simulated successes, not 16 more actual wins. The 32 cases within a draft are related and are not 32 independent PvP matches. Six generated lineups are a small, policy-dependent test population. No statistical confidence interval or real-world percentage is inferred.

Confirmation batch (seeds 4701–4706): baseline **112/192**, cross-check **107/192**; one improved, one declined, four tied. Seed 4706 dropped from 20/32 to 14/32 despite a better full-strategy training score. Across both batches: baseline 201/384, cross-check 212/384, four improvements, one decline, seven ties. The aggregate does not erase the confirmation regression. **Rejected as the Auto/default replacement.** The optional mode is explicitly experimental.

The benchmark isolates strategy selection with default own builds. It does not measure the entire item search, changing player behavior, actual PvP outcomes, or superiority to professional captains. Confirmation-batch results are recorded separately without tuning the shortlist size against them. Timings for each confirmation draft, including its held-out checks, ranged from about 21 to 42 seconds on this Node runtime; browser timing is separate.

## Release checks

Regression checks exercise native formula/payload parity, legal drafts, controls, item builds, complete and partial scenario groups, cancellation, scout conditioning, both factions and finalist ranking. Browser verification uses the native-control replay, not an authenticated match. Live accuracy needs completed configuration evidence paired with actual final match inputs/outcomes, especially hidden enemy roles and items. Until those are available, improving assumption coverage is defensible; claiming perfect prediction or guaranteed wins is not.

Verified browser preparation replay: default Quick completed 64-plan screening, checked 258 complete item candidates, and retained 55 completed retuned plans before its deadline. It applied five roles, all 30 item slots and six strategy choices, clicked Continue and observed the native PvP strategy-lock acknowledgement. The downloaded v2.6.0 report matched requested and observed inputs and retained three enemy preset assumptions with no experimental refinement. The experimental selector, scope label and regression warning were checked in the browser; completed cross-check behavior is covered by the native benchmark and regression suite. The fixture prep clock is static, so this is a control-flow check rather than proof of performance under a live countdown.
